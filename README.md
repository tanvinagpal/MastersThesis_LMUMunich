# MastersThesis_LMUMunich
Title: Contribution of Canonical NF-kB Pathway to Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia Development at Pre-tumor and Tumor Stages

Abstract:
Chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) is one of the most common types of B-Cell Lymphoma. In
CLL, CD5+ B-cells become malignant and aggregate as small, mature neoplastic lymphocytes in
the blood, marrow, and secondary lymphoid tissues. The canonical NF-kB pathway regulates proinflammatory
genes and is also implicated in CLL thus warranting its further investigation.
This study used mouse models that demonstrate the pre-tumor and tumor stages of different CLL
genotypes, with or without constitutive canonical NF-kB pathway activation. Two datasets were
obtained from RNA-sequencing. First from pre-tumor spleen and peritoneal cavity B1-cells and
second from tumor cells, for each genotype. Differential Gene Expression (DGE) analysis was
performed on both using RStudio. Three DGE methods: Deseq2, edgeR and Limma-voom were
applied on tumor dataset. Out of these three, edgeR was chosen for analyzing the pre-tumor
dataset. Next, Gene Ontology and Pathway Enrichment analyses were done for both datasets.
DGE results showed that genes which were defensive and protective against cancer (Igkv5-39,
Ighm and Mgmt) were more significant in the pre-tumor stage, while genes that promoted
proliferation and survival of cancer cells (Eef1a1, Qars and Gnai2) were more significant in the
tumor stage and were associated with NF-kB pathway.
Therefore, this study enhanced the understanding of genes which interact with the canonical NFkB
pathway and which could lead to tumor proliferation and survival in CLL, in the pre-tumor and
tumor stages. It highlighted differences between spleen and peritoneal cavity microenvironments
and their contribution to disease evolution. Further, it uncovered therapeutic targets for improving
quality of life and prognosis of CLL patients.
